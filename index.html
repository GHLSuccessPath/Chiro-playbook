The moment this is named after

Every care plan contains one visit that changes everything.

Not a number on a calendar. A transition every patient goes through.

Definition
Visit 9 is the first visit a patient attends for a reason other than pain.

Before it, they have a motivator that brings them through your door on its own. After it, they do not.

It exists on a 12 visit plan, a 24 and a 36. Where it lands in your practice is something you measure in Part 3. The number is shorthand. The transition is real.

You do a thorough Day 1. You deliver a Report of Findings, the patient understands the plan and agrees to it. They say yes and they start care.

Then the plan and the chart stop matching.

The mechanism

Two gaps. They overlap, and that is the whole problem.

Your practice has three moments that establish why a patient should keep coming. Patients fall out in the space between them.

1

Day 1

The patient arrives in pain and experiences your care.

2

Day 2 · The ROF

You establish the problem, the plan and the value. The patient commits.

3

Progress exam

Roughly monthly. Objective evidence, and the commitment renews.

Your plan length
Watch the red zone. The moment a patient starts asking whether they still need care lands inside the stretch where nothing in your practice is scheduled to answer the question. They do not raise it with you. They just start rescheduling.
Gap 1 · Structural

Nothing is scheduled between the ROF and the re-exam

They get adjusted, leave, come back. The care is happening. The case is not being reinforced. Roughly a month of visits with no formal reminder of the plan or their progress. Nobody is doing anything wrong.

Gap 2 · Clinical

The pain stops before the correction is finished

Improvement around visits 6 to 8 is your care working. It is also the moment the single motivator that brought them through your door disappears, well before the plan is complete.

Why the two together are worse than either alone

Either gap on its own is survivable. A patient with no reinforcement who is still in pain keeps coming, because the pain does the work. A patient whose pain resolves inside a well-reinforced plan usually finishes, because the plan is still in front of them.

It is the overlap that empties your schedule. Everything in Part 2 is built to occupy that space.

The three

No reason to attend beyond the appointment existing. No visibility of their own progress. And nothing at all for the patient who already stopped, because by definition they have no appointment on the books.

Reminders solve a memory problem. This is a motivation problem. Different problem, different tool.

Why a retention engine works

Three mechanisms, all well documented.

The effects are established. The size of each one in a chiropractic setting is something you measure.

MECHANISM 01

Visible progress increases completion

People finish things they can see themselves progressing through. It is why a loyalty card issued with two stamps already on it outperforms an empty one.

Goal-gradient · Kivetz et al  /  Endowed progress · Nunes & Drèze
MECHANISM 02

People work harder to avoid losing

A patient protecting an unbroken streak behaves differently from one pursuing a reward. Whether streaks beat plain points in your practice is worth measuring.

Loss aversion · Kahneman & Tversky
MECHANISM 03

Balances create switching cost

A patient carrying a meaningful balance has a reason to stay that has nothing to do with your clinical skill. Unglamorous, and it works.

Sunk cost and endowment effects
The three principles

Have these three and it works. Miss one and it does not.

1

Give them a reason to attend that is not pain

Whatever you build has to mean something to a patient who feels completely fine.

Test: if a symptom-free patient would still want to come Thursday, you built it right.
2

Make progress visible

The patient must see where they are in the plan and what they have accumulated, without asking anyone.

Test: a balance the patient never sees is a spreadsheet, not an engine.
3

Reward the behaviour, never the outcome

Reward attending, confirming, rebooking, showing up to the progress exam. Never reward getting better, and never reward saying favourable things about you.

Test: behaviour is what you can influence. It is also the compliant position. Both point the same way.

01

The Progress Exam Bridge

Highest value, easiest to implement, because the appointment already exists.
NO SOFTWARENO COSTTHIS WEEK

Your progress exam is the third retention point and the strongest one. It is the only moment in the plan where the patient sees objective evidence something changed. Most practices treat it as an administrative appointment. It should be the most important visit in the month.

01
Book it at the Report of Findings, not laterIt goes on the calendar before the patient leaves Day 2. An appointment that already exists gets attended. One scheduled "when we get closer" competes with everything else in their life.
02
Award points for attending100 points, the second-largest single award after plan completion. Skip this line if you are not running points yet.
03
Send a framing message two days beforeNot a reminder. A frame. The word "measure" is doing the work.
04
Send the findings in writing afterwardsVerbal progress is forgotten by Thursday.
Message · two days beforeSMS
Wednesday is your progress exam, not a regular visit. We re-measure everything from your first day and you'll see exactly what has changed. It takes about fifteen minutes longer than usual.
Why it works: it repositions the appointment from care they receive to evidence they get.
Message · after the examSMS or email
Good exam today. Cervical rotation up from 45 to 62 degrees on the right, and your pain scale is down from 7 to 2. The rotation is the part we're still working on, which is why we've got you through to visit 24.
A written summary is re-readable, and it is what a patient shows their spouse when asked why they are still going to the chiropractor.
02

The Relief Conversation

Forty seconds, by the doctor, at the moment nothing on a screen can help.
NO SOFTWARE40 SECONDSTHE DOC, NOT THE CA

When: at the visit before your relief window, so you arrive ahead of the decision rather than after it. You find your window in Part 3, Step 2.

01
Name what they are feeling before they say it
"You're probably feeling quite a bit better at this point."
02
Validate it
"That's exactly what should be happening right about now."
03
Separate relief from correction
"Here's the part most people don't know. Feeling better and being structurally corrected are two different finish lines, and the first one shows up a lot earlier. The pain is settling. What caused the pain isn't finished."
04
Name the risk plainly, without fear selling
"If we stop here, a lot of people are back inside the year with the same thing. If we finish the plan, fewer are."
05
Hand off to the progress exam
"Your progress exam is on the 14th and that's where we actually measure it rather than guess. Sarah's going to book the visits between now and then at the front."
Why step 5 matters more than it looks

You are not asking them to keep coming indefinitely. You are asking them to reach a specific, finite, already-scheduled measurement date. A much smaller request, and it hands them off to your strongest retention point.

Why the script works: you say the thing the patient is already privately thinking, before they say it. That makes you credible, because you predicted their own experience. And it removes the awkwardness that leads people to quietly reschedule instead of asking.

04

The Streak

Turns attendance into something the patient protects rather than schedules.
POINTS LAYER4 VISITS50 POINT BONUS
Try it · this is what the patient sees
Log four visits in a row without skipping a week.

A patient three visits into a streak who is thinking about skipping Thursday is not weighing up whether to attend an appointment. They are weighing up whether to give up a streak. Those are different decisions.

Be honest about what this measures

In a patient-reported program the streak tracks logging, not attendance. Someone who comes in and forgets to log breaks their own streak. It is weaker than an attendance-verified streak and you should know that going in. It still earns its place, because logging is the habit that keeps the plan visible to them.

01
Four is a reasonable defaultAt three visits a week that is about a week and a half. Two is too easy to mean anything. Six is too far away to feel worth protecting.
02
Nudge the log, not the visit"You've got 3 visits logged. Add this week's and your bonus lands." Highest leverage message in the program.
03
One rescue per plan, on request onlyNever automatic, never offered proactively. A patient who asks has just re-engaged.
04
Do not build attendance verificationTying the streak to the chart means your front desk checking every claim. Real work for a 50 point bonus.

06

The Reactivation Sequence

Four messages over eleven days, to the patients you already lost.
FASTEST REVENUE4 MESSAGES · 11 DAYSCHECK SMS CONSENT FIRST

Who it goes to: every patient with no visit in the last 90 days who did not complete their plan. In a practice open five years it is commonly several hundred people, and most practices are surprised by the number.

Not more than four. A patient who ignores four messages is not returning, and continuing past that creates a complaint risk.

DAY 1
The honest oneNO OFFER
Hi [First], it's [Dr. Name]'s office. Looking at your chart, you completed [X] of your [Y] visits and then we lost you. No sales pitch here, I just want to know: did the pain come back, or did life just get busy? Either answer is fine.
Why: it reads like a person rather than a practice. The one to keep if you could only send one.
DAY 4
The reasonREFRAME
Most people stop once they start feeling better, which for a lot of patients is somewhere around the sixth or eighth visit. That's the tricky part. Feeling better and being corrected happen at different times, which is why the plan ran longer than that. Nothing is lost, we can pick up where you left off.
Why: reframes their stopping as normal rather than something to feel defensive about.
DAY 8
The mechanismPRELOADED BALANCE
One thing that's new since you were last in: we started a patient rewards program. You earn points for coming in, doing your exercises and finishing your plan, and you cash them in here at the office for real things. I've already put [X] points on your account for the visits you completed. Book your next visit and they're yours to use.
Why the preload works: it is not an offer, it is something they already own and have not collected. Costs you nothing, because they genuinely did attend those visits.
DAY 11
The closeEASY OUT
Last one from me, I promise. If you'd like to come back in, reply YES and I'll find you a time. If not, no hard feelings and I hope you're doing well.
Why: converts people who ignored the first three. The finality is what prompts a decision.
◆ Before you add a free exam to message 3

Offering free or discounted care to bring a patient back is treated very differently from crediting nominal, non-cash reward points, particularly where Medicare or Medicaid patients are involved. The points version carries far less exposure and converts about as well.

Read before you build

Compliance considerations.

Not legal advice. A plain English map of where the issues sit, so the conversation with your own counsel is short, specific and inexpensive rather than exploratory.

Highest risk

Referral rewards

The federal Anti-Kickback Statute addresses remuneration intended to induce referrals of federal program business. Many states have their own statutes that apply to all patients.

Why referrals are not in the Earn List. If you want them, that is a specific conversation with an attorney in your state. Do not copy what another practice appears to be doing.
Highest risk

Incentivised reviews

Where anything of value is provided in connection with a review, FTC guidance addresses disclosure and prohibits conditioning it on the review being favourable.

A compliant version can be built, deliberately: reward the act of giving feedback regardless of sentiment, disclose the incentive, never condition on positivity.
Manage it

Inducements to federal beneficiaries

Federal law restricts remuneration to Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries likely to influence provider choice. A longstanding nominal value concept has historically been applied at levels such as $15 per item and $75 per patient annually. Confirm current figures with counsel.

Two approaches: keep every reward beneath the threshold and track annual totals, or exclude federal beneficiaries. Most prefer the first.
Manage it

Patient messaging consent

A reactivation campaign is a marketing message, not an appointment reminder, and the two are treated differently. Marketing texts require prior consent, a clear opt-out, and immediate honouring of opt-outs.

Before Component 6: check what your intake paperwork says about SMS. Many practices find their forms cover reminders but not marketing.
The lower-risk design is also the effective one

Reward attendance and self-care. Nothing else.

You are not offering remuneration to influence which provider a patient chooses. They already chose you and already committed to care you clinically recommended. What is offered is modest, non-cash encouragement to complete it. That is a materially different position from paying someone to bring you business.

It also happens to be where the money is. Referrals and reviews are useful. Plan completion is the six-figure number in Part 1.
